# M8: Data and backtester

## What was built

The research layer: real and synthetic market data, two backtesters that share one
strategy-agnostic core, the three SPEC momentum strategies, walk-forward validation, and
run records that reproduce byte for byte. Strategy orders in event mode are matched by
the real C++ engine through `mfengine`.

- **Data** (`research/data/`, `docs/data.md`):
  - Adapters for the Binance public archive (USD-M `bookTicker` and trades), Tiingo daily
    bars (key from `TIINGO_API_KEY` only) and plain CSV bars.
  - Normalized Parquet with integer prices, sizes and nanosecond timestamps, validated
    on every write and every load.
  - Download manifests that pin checksums. Nothing downloaded is committed.
- **Synthetic flow** (`research/sim/`): noise traders (Poisson arrivals), a market maker
  that skews its quotes by inventory, and momentum traders. They trade through the
  engine from one seed and produce L1 quotes and trades. A seeded daily-bar generator
  with splits and dividends covers the bar side.
- **Event-driven backtester** (`research/backtest/`):
  - a discrete-event loop around one `Engine`;
  - a historical replay that turns recorded quotes and trades into engine orders;
  - a broker that adds latency and assigns `seq_no`s in arrival order;
  - a live mode in which the strategy trades among the agents.
- **Vectorized backtester**: integer shares and P&L, signal at the close of `t` and
  trade at `t + lag` (`lag >= 1`), splits and dividends point-in-time, a cost-model
  hook.
- **Strategies** (`research/strategies/`): time-series momentum (volatility-scaled),
  cross-sectional 12-1 momentum, and intraday order-book momentum (short-horizon
  return plus imbalance).
- **Research hygiene**:
  - `check_causal`, a mechanical look-ahead test every bar run passes;
  - walk-forward folds whose results hold out-of-sample numbers only;
  - run records (config, data hashes, seed, assumptions, engine, git commit) and
    `reproduce`.
- **Tooling**:
  - `python -m research run|reproduce`;
  - `make backtest`, `make reproduce`, `make examples`, `make data-binance`,
    `make data-tiingo`;
  - three committed example runs that the suite reproduces.
- **`make sanitize` fix.** The ASan runtime was chosen from a compiler read out of the
  CMake cache with `$(shell ...)`. Make expands the whole recipe before running its
  first line, so on a fresh tree that read happened before `cmake` had configured
  `build-asan/`, and fell back to plain `c++`. With `CXX` pointing at another clang, the
  first run would preload the wrong runtime and abort, and a second run would pass. The
  read now happens in the shell when the line runs, and `asan_runtime.sh` refuses an
  empty compiler (`tests/test_makefile.py`). The M7 summary said `make sanitize` "didn't
  complete as one run". That abort could not be reproduced: `make sanitize` completed
  end to end, both from the existing trees and from a deleted `build-asan/` and
  `.venv-asan/`. This latent bug is the likeliest cause; nothing else was found.

## Read these first

1. `research/backtest/loop.py`: `Simulation`, the event loop every mode uses. `Priority`
   is the tie-break policy; `process` and `_deliver` are the whole engine interface.
2. `research/backtest/historical.py`: how quotes and trades become orders. `_quote`,
   `_shrink` (the queue model) and `on_events` (the consumed-liquidity bookkeeping).
3. `research/backtest/strategy.py`: `Context` and `MarketView`, where look-ahead is made
   unreachable.
4. `research/backtest/vectorized.py`: `run_vectorized`, the fill rules for bars.
5. `research/backtest/causality.py`: `check_causal`.
6. `research/runs.py`: `validate_config`, `run`, `reproduce`.
7. Tests in the same order: `tests/test_backtest_event.py`, `tests/test_vectorized.py`,
   `tests/test_walkforward.py`, `tests/test_runs.py`.

## Design decisions

**One event loop for replay, agents and live mode.** Recorded rows, agent wake-ups,
strategy orders and timers are all actions at a simulated time, so one ordering rule
covers them: `(time, priority, scheduling order)`. The synthetic generator and the
live mode are the same loop with different participants. Synthetic data therefore
reaches the replay path in the same format as recorded data. Participants react by
scheduling, never by calling the engine from inside a delivery, so one command's
events are fully delivered before the next command exists.

**Sequencing at arrival, not at decision.** A strategy decides at `t`, and its order
reaches the engine at `t + latency + jitter`. The `seq_no` is given at arrival, in one
stream with the replayed flow, so the journal is exactly what the engine saw. It
replays through `mf-replay` to the same hash (`events_sha256` in event results). Orders
keep their order through jitter: one session to the exchange.

**History wins ties, and trades go before quotes.** At equal timestamps, recorded
trades run first, then the recorded quotes that already reflect them, then agents, then
the strategy. A strategy order "at the same time" as a quote that removes its target
price misses it. That is conservative, and the test that pins it
(`test_latency_lets_the_market_move_first`) doubles as the latency test.

**Queue position falls out of the engine.** Historical size at a level is held by real
resting orders. Size added later queues behind earlier size, including behind the
strategy's order. Shrinking an order keeps its priority (M3's replace rule). The
queue-position model is then only one choice: which orders a size decrease comes from.
Newest first (pessimistic, the default) keeps the queue ahead of the strategy intact.
Oldest first (optimistic, opt-in) moves it up. No separate estimator can drift from
what the engine does.

**Taken liquidity stays taken.** The data never learns that the strategy lifted 20 from
an offer of 50, and keeps showing 50. The default subtracts what the strategy took at
that price until the level clears (price moves or side empties). The old behavior
(`restore`), where the next update puts it back, is opt-in. Taking real liquidity and
having it reappear would let a strategy take the same size twice.

**Quotes never trade with the strategy.** Replayed quote orders are post-only. If the
data's bid moves up to where the strategy is offering, in the data nothing traded
there. So the historical level is left out rather than crossing into the strategy.
Only recorded trades fill the strategy's resting orders.

**Look-ahead: unreachable in event mode, tested in bar mode.** Event-mode strategies
get a `Context` with no engine and no dataset (`__slots__`), and a `MarketView` holding
only what was delivered, as read-only arrays. `asof(t > now)` raises.
- **Why bar mode needs a test.** A vectorized strategy is handed the whole panel for
  speed, so nothing structural stops it reading `close[t+1]`.
- **The test.** Its weights up to `t` must be bit-identical when the bars after `t` are
  removed, and when they are replaced with garbage. The garbage version catches
  full-sample normalization, which the truncation version misses.
- **Where it runs.** Every bar run and every grid entry passes the check before use.
- **What it changed.** It shaped the cross-sectional strategy: "today is the last
  trading day of the month" needs tomorrow's date, so it rebalances on the first bar of
  the new month.
- **Execution.** The lag between signal and trade is `>= 1` by construction (a
  validation error, not a convention).

**Unadjusted bars.** Back-adjusted prices are rewritten by every later split and
dividend, so a 2010 price depends on 2020. Storing prices as traded plus the day's
actions keeps the dataset point-in-time. Total returns are built forward from it, and
the bar backtester applies splits (with cash in lieu) and dividends on their ex-dates.

**Integers everywhere money is.** Prices come in as decimal text and go through
`Decimal` onto the instrument's grid (off-grid is an error, not a rounding). Tiingo's
JSON is parsed with numbers as strings. Bars are in micro-dollars, and positions,
cash, P&L and equity are int64. Floats appear only in signals, sizing and summary
statistics.

**Walk-forward results hold no in-sample numbers.** `FoldResult` holds the fold and the
chosen parameters, and `WalkForwardResult` holds out-of-sample P&L and metrics. The
selector is handed `panel.upto(train_end - 1)`. Reporting in-sample performance would
mean computing it outside the API. The noise-strategy test shows what this prevents:
- in each training window, the best of 40 random strategies has an average Sharpe
  above 1.5;
- out of sample the same procedure stays within three standard errors of zero.

**Reproducibility means equal bytes.**
- **No wall clock.** No wall-clock time anywhere.
- **Canonical output.** Results are canonical JSON with floats at 12 significant digits.
- **Pinned data.** Datasets are pinned by a content hash of their logical content, not
  their Parquet bytes. Generated data is regenerated and must hash the same.
- **Deterministic generation.** The synthetic bar generator uses only `+`, `*` and
  rounding, so it does not depend on a platform's `exp`.
- **Refusals.** `reproduce` refuses if a dataset or the engine version changed; other
  differences (git commit, build type, platform) are reported.
- **Proof across builds.** The committed example runs were produced on the release
  install. The suite reproduces them on the dev install (invariant walk on), and CI
  reproduces them on Linux.

**Conservative defaults are recorded, not just documented.** Every run record's
`assumptions` carries:
- the queue model and the consumed-liquidity model;
- latency, jitter and the tie-break;
- depth (L1), the marking rule and the cost model;
- for bars: lag, fill price, sizing, rebalancing rule and the survivorship note of the
  data.

**Data sources chosen by what can be pinned.** Binance publishes a SHA-256 next to each
immutable daily archive, which is exactly a download-and-verify script. Tiingo has no
immutable archive. The manifest instead pins the content hash of each ticker's
normalized bars over a fixed range, written once with `--update-pins`, so a vendor
revision fails loudly. Stooq (a JavaScript bot wall) and the LOBSTER samples (download
links now behind a JavaScript app) could not be scripted.

## Trade-offs and what was left out

- **Gross only.** `ZeroCost` is the one cost model. M9 fills in fees, spread, slippage
  and impact behind `CostModel`.
- **L1 only.** No free, redistributable L2/L3 source could be scripted. A strategy that
  sweeps past the top level finds nothing, and replayed tape trades that printed inside
  the spread (the trades file and the book ticker are not perfectly aligned) find
  nothing to hit.
- **Nobody reacts to the strategy in replay.** This is the basic limit of replaying
  history. Live mode has reacting agents, but they are simple.
- **No market-data latency.** The strategy sees an update at the time it happened; only
  orders are delayed.
- **Python speed.** The event loop calls `Engine.process` once per command (orders
  depend on the previous command's result), so Python overhead dominates. A recorded
  day replays in seconds on the release install, but no benchmark was run this
  milestone. Batching the replay between strategy actions is the obvious next step if
  it matters.
- **Bar fills are optimistic about size:** the whole order fills at the open. M9's
  impact model is where that gets a price.
- **Sizing off fixed capital** (no compounding) keeps P&L additive and integer, and
  makes walk-forward folds comparable.
- **Example results are synthetic.** The bar generator's drift is a persistent AR(1)
  process, so momentum works on it by construction. The committed walk-forward results
  show that the pipeline runs, not that momentum works. Real conclusions need the
  Tiingo data, and M9's costs.
- **Tiingo pins are not committed yet.** They need the key; the first
  `make data-tiingo UPDATE_PINS=1` writes them.
- **Byte-identical across platforms is an expectation, not a guarantee.** Integer
  outputs and canonical float formatting make it hold in practice (CI reproduces the
  macOS-made examples on Linux). A last-bit difference in a platform's math library
  could still flip a float comparison in a signal.

## Interview questions

1. *How do you stop a backtest from using future data?* Look-ahead is made
   unreachable, not forbidden by convention.
   - **Event mode:** the strategy only receives what has been delivered, through a view
     that raises on any time after now, and it never holds the dataset or the engine.
   - **Bar mode:** whole-panel computation is needed for speed, so every strategy must
     give bit-identical weights up to `t` when the future is removed or replaced with
     garbage.
   - **Execution:** a signal trades at `t + 1` or later, by a config validation.
2. *Your strategy's resting order is in the book during a replay. When does it fill?*
   Only when a recorded trade reaches it: a tape order at a better price, or at its
   price once the size queued ahead is gone. Replayed quotes are post-only and cannot
   fill it. Queue position is the engine's own price-time priority. The one assumption
   is which orders a size decrease cancels, newest first by default, which is the
   pessimistic choice.
3. *What can't a replay backtest model?* Reaction. Nobody in the data saw your orders:
   no impact on others, no one stepping in front, liquidity you take reappearing in the
   data (here it stays removed by default). With L1 data there is also no depth and no
   true queue position. Live mode against reacting agents covers some of this, with
   toy agents.
4. *Why store unadjusted prices?* Adjusted series are recomputed whenever a new split
   or dividend happens, so an old adjusted price encodes the future. Unadjusted prices
   plus the day's corporate actions are what was known at the time. Total returns are
   built forward from them, and positions are adjusted on the ex-date.
5. *What does survivorship bias do to a momentum backtest on today's ETF list?* It
   leaves out funds that closed: the losers on the long side (bought on strength,
   later collapsed) and the winning shorts (weak names that went to zero). Both flatter
   momentum. Membership here is point-in-time for listings, through the panel's
   `valid` mask, and every record carries the dataset's survivorship note. Removing the
   bias needs delisted securities, which free data does not have.
