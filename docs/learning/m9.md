# M9: Costs and analytics

## What was built

Transaction costs, the analytics that compare gross and net, a tearsheet per run, and a
showcase of the two momentum strategies on real ETF data with every parameter fixed
before the first run.

- **Cost models** (`research/backtest/costs.py`), each a `[costs]` sub-table that can be
  switched off:
  - fees: `commission` (per share and bps), `maker_taker` (by liquidity flag, rebates
    negative), `sec_fee` (Section 31, a dated schedule with a source for every rate);
  - `spread` (half-spread bps);
  - slippage: `slippage` (fixed bps), `vol_slippage` (`k * vol`), `impact` (square root:
    `y * vol * sqrt(notional / ADV)`), `latency` (`k * vol * sqrt(latency / session)`);
  - `financing`: borrow on short value, a spread over the risk-free rate on debit cash.
- **The risk-free rate** (`research/data/fred.py`, `research/backtest/rates.py`): FRED
  DTB3, pinned like Tiingo, converted exactly from a discount yield to a calendar-day
  rate, forward-filled only.
- **Bar backtester, gross and net**: gross is frictionless (fills at the print,
  dividends, risk-free interest on every cash balance); net subtracts each component per
  bar, so `net = gross - sum(components)` exactly. Windows end flat. Walk-forward
  selects on net excess Sharpe.
- **Event backtester**: fees only, plus implementation shortfall measured from the
  mids at decision and at arrival (the broker records both).
- **Analytics** (`research/analytics/metrics.py`): returns, volatility, Sharpe,
  Sortino, Calmar, drawdown depth and duration, beta and alpha, episode hit rate, average
  win and loss, profit factor, FIFO holding period, turnover, exposure, implementation
  shortfall, cost breakdown and drag, Sharpe standard errors (i.i.d. and with skew and
  kurtosis), probabilistic and deflated Sharpe, and a capacity curve.
- **Tearsheet** (`research/analytics/report.py`, `tearsheet.py`): every run writes
  `metrics.json` and `tearsheet.html` (gross and net side by side, inline SVG, no
  scripts), both hashed in the record and compared by `reproduce`.
- **Showcase** (`research/showcase/`): time-series and cross-sectional momentum on the
  22-ETF Tiingo universe, 2007 to 2024, walk-forward, out of sample only.
- **Fixes found along the way**:
  - Tiingo dividends have up to 8 decimals: dividends are now in 1e-8 dollars, exact;
  - the downloader wrote an unsorted universe (the first real fetch failed validation);
  - a target filled on a split's ex-date was sized in pre-split shares.

How to read it:
- Neither strategy is distinguishable from zero after costs at the usual bar: with a
  standard error of 0.27, tsmom's net Sharpe of 0.24 is under one standard error, and
  the deflated Sharpe (34.8%) says the best of six zero-skill parameter sets would often
  do as well. xsmom's net Sharpe is 0.03.
- Costs take about a third of tsmom's gross excess return and 70% of xsmom's. The
  spread is tsmom's largest cost (it trades its volatility-scaled sizes almost daily);
  borrow is xsmom's (half its book is short, all the time).
- Capacity: tsmom's net Sharpe is gone by $100M; at $1B and up, single fills are several
  times a day's volume (no fill is capped), so those points only say "does not scale",
  and gross Sharpe there differs because selection at that size picks slower parameters.
- The universe is survivorship-biased (ETFs alive today), which flatters both.

## Design decisions

**Event mode charges fees only.** A fill from the engine's book already paid the spread:
a marketable buy fills at the ask, and the position is marked at the bid. The queue and
the latency are in it too. Adding a spread or impact model would charge them twice, so
building one for event mode is an error that says why. What the book cost is still
reported, as measurement: the broker notes the mid when the strategy decides and when
the order arrives; `delay` is the move between them and `spread_paid` each fill against
the arrival mid. A test does a round trip across a 2-tick spread: gross is exactly
`-spread * qty`, costs are only the commission, and the measured `spread_paid` equals
what gross lost.

**Bar mode charges the half-spread.** A bar fill is at the open or close print, which is
a trade price with no spread in it. Every bar fill is treated as a taker (taker fees,
half-spread paid), the conservative reading of an order that must fill at the open.

**Point-in-time impact inputs.** Volatility and ADV for a fill on bar `e` come from bars
`e - 20 .. e - 1`. At the open of `e` that day's volume does not exist yet; M8's
`TradeRecord.volume` is that day's volume and stays in the record, but no cost reads
it. ADV is in dollars, `volume * close` of the same unadjusted bar, so a split (twice
the shares at half the price) does not move it, where share ADV would jump. The inputs
are rolling sums over earlier bars; a test changes the fill bar and every later bar and
sees no change, and changes the bar before and does see one.

**The risk-free rate is DTB3, converted exactly.** A 3-month bill quoted at a discount
yield `d` costs `1 - 91d/360` and pays 1. Its holding return per calendar day is
`d / (360 - 91d)`, which in basis points is the fraction `bp / (3_600_000 - 91 bp)`:
interest on integer cash stays exact. Each bar accrues the calendar days since the
previous bar (Fridays to Mondays earn three) at the last rate observed on or before the
previous bar: a holiday carries the last known rate forward and nothing is filled
backward, so a bar before the first observation is an error.

**Gross includes the risk-free rate.** In a frictionless world cash earns the risk-free
rate and a debit pays it. Making that part of gross keeps excess-return Sharpe
consistent: a flat book has zero excess return, gross and net. What is above the
risk-free rate (borrow on shorts, a spread on debit cash) is a cost. So is
`carry_on_costs`: money spent on costs stops earning interest.

**Exact accounting.** Net is gross minus every component on every bar, in integers. The
tests check `gross - costs == net` on every run, and that the cost breakdown, the yearly
breakdown and the shortfall parts all add up.

**Windows end flat.** Each walk-forward window starts flat, so without a liquidation the
entry trades are charged and the exits never are. The window's open positions are sold
at its last close, at the mark, so gross does not change; only the costs do.

**Selection on net.** Costs are known before trading, so the walk-forward picks the
best net excess Sharpe on the training window. A test builds a churning strategy that
wins gross and loses net, and checks which one each selection picks.

**Annualization and Sharpe.** Returns are P&L over the fixed starting capital (positions
are sized off it, no compounding). Annual return is the arithmetic mean times 252, which
matches fixed-capital P&L and needs no `pow`, so results are identical on macOS and
Linux. Volatility and Sharpe use the sample standard deviation; Sharpe uses excess
returns. Sortino's downside deviation is a target semideviation over all periods (N in
the denominator). Undefined ratios are `null`, never infinite.

**Uncertainty.** The Sharpe standard error is shown two ways: i.i.d. normal (Lo) and with
the sample skew and kurtosis (Mertens/Opdyke); the second matches a bootstrap on skewed
returns in a test. The probabilistic Sharpe is P(true SR > 0). The deflated Sharpe
raises the bar to the expected best of N zero-skill trials, N being the grid size. The
tearsheet lists the trials: grid size, folds, backtests run, capacity re-runs.

**Episodes, not trades, for hit rate.** Volatility-scaled positions are resized almost
daily, so a "trade" is not a bet. An episode is a run of bars a symbol is held with one
sign. Per bar, the backtester splits each symbol's P&L into the position held into the
bar (to the fill) and the position after the trades (from the fill), so a flip's bar
splits exactly between the episode it closes and the one it opens.

**The tearsheet is a function of `metrics.json`.** No scripts, no timestamps, no
external resources: `render(metrics)` is the file, so a reproduced run reproduces it byte
for byte, and the page can be regenerated from the JSON alone. Hover uses SVG `<title>`
tooltips; every chart has a table. Colors are the reference palette's first two slots
(gross blue, net orange), with dark-mode steps.

**Portfolio-level only.** Tiingo's terms forbid redistributing their data, and a
per-symbol daily position or P&L series would let anyone rebuild the prices.
`check_portfolio_level` refuses a key named after a symbol and any long array other than
the four portfolio series. Every run passes through it, and a test checks the committed
showcase outputs with it.

**Parameters fixed in git before the first run.** The showcase configs were committed
(`0c8f73a`) before either strategy touched the real data. The grids are the M8 ones;
the costs are the approved ones. `research/showcase/TRIALS.md` lists every real-data run.

## Trade-offs and what was left out

- **A single uniform half-spread** (2 bp) for every ETF in every year. It is generous
  for SPY and probably low for DBC or for 2008. There are no quotes in daily bars;
  estimating spreads from highs and lows (Corwin-Schultz) was left out.
- **Square-root impact with Y = 1** and 20-day inputs, from the literature, not
  calibrated. Fills are never capped by participation; it is reported instead.
- **Every bar fill takes liquidity.** There is no model of limit orders or the auction.
- **The SEC fee is per trade at the notional**, not aggregated per day by the broker.
  The pre-2007 rate's start date is not verified (it only covers bars before
  2007-03-17, before any out-of-sample bar).
- **Financing is portfolio-level**: borrow at a flat 50 bp, no hard-to-borrow names, no
  margin limits. Financing and carry are not allocated to episodes.
- **Liquidating at each window's end** costs one round trip per year that a continuous
  strategy would not pay. Carrying positions across folds is the alternative.
- **The Sharpe standard error ignores autocorrelation**, and the deflated Sharpe uses the
  null variance `1/(T-1)` because in-sample scores are deliberately never kept.
- **Event-mode runs have no Sharpe**: one session is not a return series.
- **Tearsheets are files.** Storage in Postgres is M10.

## Interview questions

1. *Your event backtest fills against a real book. Why not add a spread cost?*
   The fill price already is the ask or the bid, and the open position is marked at the
   exit side, so gross has paid the spread. A spread model would charge it twice. Event
   mode charges fees only; the spread is measured as implementation shortfall and labeled
   as inside gross.

2. *How do you estimate impact without look-ahead?*
   Volatility and ADV for a fill on day `e` come only from days `e - 20` to `e - 1`. An
   open fill cannot know that day's volume. ADV is in dollars (volume times the same
   bar's unadjusted close), so splits do not distort it. A test changes the fill day and
   later and checks the inputs do not move.

3. *What risk-free rate do you use, and how does a quoted yield become a daily rate?*
   DTB3, the 3-month bill on a discount basis. Price is `1 - 91d/360`, so a calendar day
   earns `d / (360 - 91d)`, exactly a fraction in basis points. Each bar earns the
   calendar days since the last bar at the last known rate, forward-filled only.

4. *How do you report how many things you tried?*
   Every tearsheet has a trials table (grid size, folds, backtests, capacity re-runs).
   The deflated Sharpe uses the grid size as N. Showcase parameters were committed
   before the first real run, and a ledger lists every run on real data.

5. *Why is the net Sharpe a different number from the gross Sharpe minus costs?*
   Costs change the return series, not just its mean: they land on trading days and
   cluster when the strategy turns over, which adds volatility. Selection is also on
   net, so the parameters chosen for net can differ from the gross-best ones.
